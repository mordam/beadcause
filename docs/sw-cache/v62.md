# v62 — one status filter over the epic board, and the row it draws to hold a branch up (bc-rfnr.9.6)

The board's trees are filtered now: one control between the heading and the cards, **Not
closed · All · Closed**, defaulting to not closed. Two files move together.
`public/app.js` draws the chips, narrows every card's `tree` through `p0Visible`, and
marks any row it kept *only* so the rows under it keep their place; `public/style.css`
adds `.p0-status` for where the switch sits and `.p0-row.via` for what that mark looks
like — a dashed leading edge.

**A v61 stylesheet beside a v62 app.js is the pair that matters, and it is quiet in the
way [v59](v59.md) was.** The chips themselves are fine: they are `.chip` inside
`.chip-row.scopes`, both of which v61 already has, so the segmented switch lays out and
works. What v61 has no rule for is `.p0-row.via` — and that is the only thing on screen
saying why an open bead is in the tree while the filter says `Closed`. Without it those
ancestors draw as ordinary matched rows, so the tree under a `Closed` filter reads as a
list of open beads: not a broken page, a page arguing that the filter does not work. The
mark is the whole of the answer to "why is this row here", and it is exactly the kind of
missing rule nobody reports as a stylesheet problem.

The other way round is harmless. A v62 stylesheet over a v61 app.js styles two class
names nothing draws, and the board is what it was.

`test/swbump.mjs` reads this pair and calls it advisory rather than red, which is correct
on its own terms — nothing calls a member the other half lacks, so nothing throws. The
argument for bumping anyway is the one above: the failure is legibility, and a filter that
looks like it is not working is a control people stop using.

Nothing else on the page is affected. The pick lives in `state.p0status` and
`localStorage`, it is read only by the board's own renderers, and `underOwnedP0s` — what
narrows the inbox list under the board — never reads it: a bead filtered out of a tree is
still a question in the list. The fetch handler is network-first, so this pair only exists
on an offline or flapping load and heals itself on the next online one.
