# v45 — a ticket row that says what became of it, drawn by a stylesheet that has never heard of it (bc-0i27.5)

This is the v32 pairing, not v44's. There is no member and no call: `app.js` gained
`jiraIngestHtml`, which appends a `<div class="jira-ingest">` under each JIRA ticket row
saying whether the ticket has been read yet, and `style.css` gained the four rules that
draw it. Nothing throws. `test/swbump.mjs` will have said *advisory* and nothing more,
which is exactly what it says about every bump from v32 to v40.

What a v44 stylesheet does with a v45 `app.js` is worse than "unstyled", though, and that
is why this is a bump rather than a skip. `.jira-card` stopped being a flex **row** in
this release and became a flex **column** — that is the whole of how the bead's id gets to
sit *under* the ticket instead of inside it, which it has to, because the row is a link to
JIRA and the id is a link to the bead and an `<a>` may not contain an `<a>`. A phone
holding v44's stylesheet applies `display: flex; align-items: center` to a card that now
has two children: the ticket row and the ingestion line get laid out **side by side**,
the row squeezed into whatever is left, with `flex: 1` still on `.work-row` and no
`gap`. Every JIRA ticket in the inbox draws as a squashed row with a fragment of grey
text jammed against its right edge, on top of the ordinary unstyled-control problem —
`.jira-ingest` itself has no size, no colour and no indent, so the failure state
(`could not be read — …`) arrives in body text with nothing marking it as a failure.

The mild half is the one that matters least here and is worth naming anyway: a v44 phone
would draw the whole thing in the same weight as the ticket title, so *reading the
ticket…* would read as part of the summary.

Network-first means this is an offline or flapping-link load and self-heals online. It is
still the state a phone comes back to after a day in a bag, on the one screen this epic
exists to put something useful on.
